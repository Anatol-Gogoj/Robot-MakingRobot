# ADR-0008: The control box is a 2020 frame in two modules, not off-the-shelf enclosures
Status: Accepted; its rail source, plate materials and PE scheme are superseded by ADR-0009 (2026-10-17); Date: 2026-10-15 (owner decision); Scope: `docs/wiring` sheets 3 and 4, the deck plate DXFs, `bom_frame.csv`
Context: The control panel needs two decks: 110 VAC and 24 V power below, drives and logic above. Each deck's parts span about 409 x 284 mm and stand 125 mm tall (sheet 3), inside an 18 x 13 x 11 in envelope set by the owner. Two ways to house them were costed on 2026-10-15.
Decision: Keep the 2020 frame (Misumi HFS5-2020 cut to order) with 1/8 in 6061-T6 deck plates, built as two modules: the upper module (mid ring, upper plate, upper posts, lid ring) is wired on the bench and drops onto four locating studs in the lower posts (sheet 4).
Rejected: Two indoor NEMA 1 steel enclosures with hinged doors and back panels (20 x 16 x 8 in for power, 20 x 16 x 6 in for drives and logic). The 17 x 13 in back panels hold each deck's layout nearly unchanged, and the boxes bring doors, ground studs and full mains segregation. Distributor prices on 2026-10-15: $167 to $184 per 20 x 16 x 6 NEMA 1 hinged box (Saginaw), $51 per back panel (AutomationDirect NP2016); McMaster's own listings could not be read that day. They need about 32 x 20 in of wall instead of the 18 x 13 x 11 in envelope, a filtered fan per box, and they cannot stack flat because the lower door would be covered.
Rejected: A single deck plate held in the 2020 slots (the ~302 mm plate): rattles in the slot, goes in only with an end rail off, and anodized slot walls give no PE bond.
Consequences: The owner fabricates and wires the frame: Misumi cut list with end tapping, two plates cut from 24 x 24 in sheets (or laser from the DXFs), side and top panels still to design. The deck layouts on sheet 3 carry over to back panels if the enclosures are ever chosen.
Revisit if: the box moves off the 18 x 13 x 11 in envelope to a wall or machine-frame mount, the side panels plus Misumi rail cost more than two NEMA 1 boxes with back panels and fans, or the frame build stalls for lack of shop time.
