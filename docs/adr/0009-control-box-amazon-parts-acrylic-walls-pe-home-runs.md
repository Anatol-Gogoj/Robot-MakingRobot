# ADR-0009: The control box is built from Amazon parts: shop-cut 6 mm-slot 2020, 1/16 in aluminum decks, acrylic walls, every metal part home-run to PE
Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-10-17); Date: 2026-10-17 (owner direction: Amazon hardware, 6 mm Euro-standard rail, a laser-cut plastic enclosure, every separate metal part bonded to PE); Scope: `docs/wiring` sheets 3 and 4, `bom_frame.csv`, `tools/draw_wiring.py`; supersedes the material and sourcing parts of ADR-0008
Context: The 2026-10-15 frame BoM priced $566 (Misumi rail $146, two 24 x 24 in 6061 sheets $184, McMaster screws) with no side panels and no PE bonding. The owner wants the cost pushed down: Amazon hardware, a 6 mm-slot Euro 2020 rail that takes the same T-nuts as the OpenBuilds V-slot gantry (Rat Rig lists V-slot 2020 with a 6 mm slot), and a laser-cut plastic enclosure with every separate metal part bonded to PE. McMaster's 20 mm rail, 5537T101, has a 5 mm slot (read on its page 2026-10-17), so it cannot share that hardware.
Decision: Buy the rail as ten 1000 mm IXGNIJ sticks and cut the 20 pieces in the shop from six of them. Buy the brackets, roll-in spring T-nuts, screws, feet, sheet stock, wire and lugs on Amazon; only the slotted duct stays at McMaster. The owner supplies the ground bars, the M5 hand tap and part of the 12 AWG PE wire from stock. Both decks are 1/16 in 5052 ($23.81 a sheet), sheared, then drilled and nibbled through a template cut from acrylic on the laser; the plate DXFs are unchanged. The lower back panel, which carries the inlet and Q0, comes from the base plate's sheet, so no mains part sits on plastic. The side and top panels are 1/8 in cast acrylic cut on the laser. Anodize insulates, so each rail is its own part: one tapped M5 bond hole per rail, and one 12 AWG home run per metal part to its module's ground bar on its plate's PE stud (bars of 15 terminals or more: one below, two above, one on the gantry); DIN rails bond through their PE terminals.
Rejected: McMaster rail (5 mm slot). Misumi cut to order ($146 against $75 for the sticks). An acrylic deck: the upper plate is the ceiling of the 110 VAC compartment, 3 mm over the 1200 W PSU, and cast acrylic is UL 94 HB and keeps burning once lit (Perspex cast acrylic datasheet); 1/4 in acrylic also cost more than the aluminum ($28.99 against $23.81). Polycarbonate panels: it cuts poorly on a CO2 laser, discolors and catches fire (UW Fab Lab approved-materials list). 14 AWG jumpers: 2.08 mm2 is below the 2.5 mm2 floor for a separate, protected PE conductor in IEC 60364-5-54 543.1.3; 12 AWG is 3.31 mm2. Daisy-chained bonds: removing one part would open the bond of every part after it. Bonding through T-nuts, brackets or plate screws: they all bear on anodize.
Consequences: Priced lines $443.42 on 2026-10-17, without the owner's bars, tap and wire on hand: the box $394.45 including the panels, duct and PE parts, the gantry PE $48.97. The acrylic nests on three 24 x 18 in sheets, each inside the owner's 813 x 457 mm laser bed. Shop work: 20 cuts against stops, end taps, 20 bond holes, shearing two plates and drilling and nibbling them through the template. Thinner plates move the mid ring and upper posts (upper post 128.2 mm, 50 mm of wire room over the drivers). Screw lengths rest on an assumed slot depth (6.0), lip (1.8) and bracket leg (3.5) until the first stick and bracket are measured; `ScrewFits()` refuses a length that would bottom or catch fewer than three threads. The panels have no vents or cable entries yet, and the gantry's bond count waits for a continuity survey.
Revisit if: the first stick's slot depth or lip differs from the assumed values; or the gantry survey finds a mains-powered part on a carriage.
