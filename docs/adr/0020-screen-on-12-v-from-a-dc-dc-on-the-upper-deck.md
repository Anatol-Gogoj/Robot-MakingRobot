# ADR-0020: The operator panel's screen runs on 12 V from an isolated DC-DC on F1-3, standing on the upper deck behind cable slot 2
Status: Proposed (issue #220); amends ADR-0016's "the screen runs on the Pi" and its spare F1-3; Date: 2026-11-12; Scope: `tools/draw_wiring.py` (sheets 1, 2A to 2D, 3 and 4, the BoM), `tools/layout_study/` (block PS12), `CAD/Control Box`, `docs/wiring`
Context: ADR-0016 has the screen running on the Pi, so sheet 1 drew the 10.1 in screen fed "from the Pi" and left F1-3 spare. On 2026-11-11 the owner found that the screen takes 12 V on a DC barrel jack, marked "DC-12V" on its rear housing beside its Type-C, HDMI and audio ports. The screen's label reads "Input: DC 12V 2A". Its adapter (Guangdong Sangu SG-120300050U) is rated 12.0 V, 3.0 A, 36.0 W, center positive. Both readings come from the owner's photos of the labels, 2026-11-12. The box offers +24 V (FB1 always on while K1 is in, FB2 behind K2) and the Pi's 5 V PSU, and nothing at 12 V. The decks follow the packing study (ADR-0010), and the lower deck has no rail space left: its open spots lie under cable slot 2, around the base PE stud, and in the lower left grommet's lane. The upper deck has one free spot, 72 x 99 mm between the shield clamp and the back run, behind slot 2.
Decision: A Mean Well DDR-60G-12 DIN-rail DC-DC converter fills that spot (DDR-60-SPEC, 2026-03-31): 9 to 36 V DC in; 12 V, 5 A, 60 W out; output adjustable 9 to 13.2 V; 4 kV DC isolation between input and output; 52.5 x 90 x 54.5 mm on TS35. It stands on its own 66.5 mm rail along x (block PS12, center x 339.25, y 205.5). The rail reaches 7 mm past each end of the converter, room for the rail's USLKG 5 ground block, and stops 1 mm short of the clamp. Wiring: F1-3 (3 A) feeds +Vin, and -Vin returns to star S on its own wire. Both go up slot 2 on the 20 AWG on hand, as for FB1's other 3 A loads under ADR-0019. The 12 V pair runs about 40 mm along the motor duct beside the shielded motor cables, comes back down slot 2 and leaves by the lower left grommet beside the Pi's 5 V feed. It is a Tensility 10-02226 pigtail: 6 ft of 2-conductor 18 AWG ending in a right-angle 5.5 x 2.5 mm plug, the screen's size (owner, 2026-11-12), center positive (red), rated 48 V and 7 A on its maker's drawing, above the converter's 6.75 A current limit. The output's 0 V is not tied to 0 V or PE in the box. The Pi's HDMI and USB reference it to the box's 0 V, so the screen's return current flows back through the converter alone and not through those grounds. The isolation is not a safety barrier. Sizing: the supply is sized for the adapter's 3 A, above the screen's own 2 A. The converter's 5 A and 60 W are 1.67 times the adapter's rating and 2.5 times the screen's. At 36 W the input draws 36 / 0.91 / 22 V = 1.80 A, 60 % of F1-3's 3 A. At the screen's 24 W it draws 1.20 A. The 0.91 is the datasheet efficiency; 22 V is a conservative floor at the converter's input after FB1 and the slot 2 run. The pigtail drops 2 x 1.83 m x 26.1 mOhm/m = 0.29 V at 3 A (2.4 %) and 0.19 V at 2 A. That is 18 AWG stranded copper at 7.95 ohm per 1000 ft and 75 C, NEC Chapter 9 Table 8. The output trim covers it. F1-3 sits at the converter's typical full-load input of 3 A at 24 V. An output overload into the converter's current limit (105 to 135 %, 63 to 81 W, or 2.9 to 4.0 A in at 22 V) therefore opens F1-3 before or with the limit. The margin above belongs to the converter, not to the branch.
Rejected: The converter on the lower deck beside the 5 V PSU, the brief's starting point: there is no rail space there without moving the 5 V PSU, the servo PSU or the base PE stud, and with them the lanes into slot 2 and the lower left grommet. The converter at the operator panel, fed 24 V through the lower left grommet: it would halve the cable current, but nothing there is 90 mm deep: the plate is ringed by the controls, the display cradle and the monitor arm's plate, and the plugs leave the display's left end into the only clear space. It would also put a fused-box part on a moving arm. The screen's own wall adapter: a second mains cord at the panel, and a screen that stays lit with K1 out. A non-isolated buck: the screen's return current would split between its 0 V wire and the HDMI and USB grounds. A boost from the 5 V PSU: ADR-0016 has not shown that the 5 V PSU even holds the Pi 5.
Consequences: Wiring: four more conductors pass slot 2, and the lower left grommet carries the pigtail. Packing study: block PS12 and nets RFB-PS12 1, PS12-R24 1 and PS12-LL 2 are added, and the layout rescores from 36839 to 38304 conductor-mm. No other block moves. Parts that follow from the new rail: one more DIN rail piece (11 pieces, 1881 mm, still from the five 18 in rails on hand), one more USLKG 5 ground block (15 of the pack of 20), one more PE home run on the upper deck (27), and two more M4 rail screws. Unchanged: the deck plates and their shop drawings, since rail holes are drilled at assembly. BoM: the converter ($43.82 at TRC Electronics, read 2026-11-12) and the pigtail ($6.57 at Digi-Key, read 2026-11-12). E-stop: on FB1, the screen stays lit through an E-stop, as the Pi and the tower do, and goes dark when K1 drops. EMC: the converter's emissions are Class A, an industrial rating, with Class A tested on 1 m leads and the pigtail 1.83 m. If the screen or the Pi's USB shows interference, fit a clip-on ferrite at the converter end. NFPA 79: the NFPA 79 version on record (ADR-0019) wants 14 AWG copper on power circuits (12.6.1), and the 18 AWG pigtail departs from it. Open: the screen's measured current; the pigtail's route and length from the grommet to the panel; the right-angle plug's fit at the display's left end in the panel CAD (issue #219); and the blade fuse's I2t against the converter's 20 A typical inrush, likely harmless since FB1 rises with the PSU when K1 closes.
Revisit if: the screen turns out to take its power through its Type-C port, the deck layout is solved again, the panel gains a 90 mm deep spot for a converter, or the screen's measured draw stays far below its 2 A and a smaller converter is wanted.
