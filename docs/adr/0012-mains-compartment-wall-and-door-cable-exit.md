# ADR-0012: A bent 5052 wall closes the 110 VAC compartment, and the 110 V door cable leaves through the back panel
Status: Proposed (2026-10-18, for the owner); Date: 2026-10-18; Scope: `tools/draw_wiring.py` (the wall's constants, `WallFlat()`, `WallChecks()`, `CornerGap()`, `DeckSolids()`, `DoorGland`), `docs/wiring` sheets 3 to 5, `deck_base_plate.dxf`, `panel_lower_back_template.dxf`, `wall_mains_flat.dxf`, `bom_frame.csv`; issues #146 and #152
Context: ADR-0010 closed the mains compartment with a wall 10 mm outside the mains parts, but sheet 3 drew it only as a line and the BoM listed it as not designed. Two sets of conductors must leave the compartment. The PSU's DC leads go to K2's rail and the fuse rail. X1 sends a 110 VAC cable to the door plate (F0 through the S1 knob to K1's coil, the H1 lamp, the plate PE: sheets 1 and 2), and the packing study's nets leave that cable out.
Decision: Two pieces of 1/16 in 5052, cut from the upper plate sheet's offcut. Each is bent once along its foot into a 14 mm flange on the low-voltage side and screwed to the base plate with M4 x 12 and nyloc nuts. A tab bent at wall B's front end is screwed to wall A's mains face. B is folded flange first and the tab after it, with the folded flange hanging past the end of the brake. So the tab and its bend start 16 mm over the deck, 12.8 mm above the top of the flange's bend, and B's foot stays open 5.2 x 16 mm between A's mains face and the start of B's flange (issue #152). Each piece ends in a tab that reaches between the bottom and mid rings to 2 mm from the back and right panels. A and B stop 1 mm short of the rings. B's tab and a notch in the top of its right end keep 1 mm from the front right corner brackets and their M5 heads, modelled as 20 mm legs with filled triangles until a bracket is measured (`BracketChecks()`; the review of #148 found the first tab 1.5 mm into two of them). The top stops 3 mm under the upper plate: its 0.9 mm sag under 5 kg, a 0.5 mm short post, and 1 mm, checked by `WallChecks()`. A 40 x 13 mm edge-trimmed pass-through in wall A, centred on the mains duct, takes the DC leads. The 110 V door cable leaves the compartment straight through an M20 cable gland in the aluminum back panel. Barrier rule: no opening in the wall passes the IP2X probes of IEC 60529, a 12.5 mm sphere and the 12 mm jointed test finger. `CornerGap()` measures the thickest rod through B's open corner in the flat pattern, 5.6 mm, and holds it under 12 mm. As drawn, every other gap the wall leaves is 3 mm or less, except the pass-through. That opening is 13 mm high before its edge trim, and the trim must bring it under 12 mm.
Rejected: Crossing the wall with the door cable and leaving with the E-stop loop through the lower left grommet: it runs a mains cable through the low-voltage side and shares a grommet with SELV cables. An acrylic wall: cast acrylic is UL 94 HB, the reason ADR-0009 keeps every deck around the mains metal. The spare 2020 corner brackets as feet: they fix the wall at points, leave the foot open, and put cast brackets on 1.6 mm sheet. One wall bent into an L: three bends in one 528 mm blank on a small brake. Closing B's corner in the flat pattern (issue #152). The tab and its bend would run down to 2 mm over the deck, with a 2 mm relief slot, 5.2 mm tall, at the flange. That shrinks the opening to a 2.4 mm rod, but the corner bend then runs down beside the flange's bend. Neither bend can then be made second on a hand brake: the brake's end would have to fall in a window of 1 to 2 mm, which takes a press brake with segmented dies. IP2X does not call for the change, since the open corner keeps both probes out. If the PSU's terminals face the 110 V rail, the nearest mains terminal is about 225 mm from the opening.
Consequences: The base plate takes six flange holes, drilled through the wall in place, and its PE stud moves to (330, 150) to clear the flange. Two more PE jumpers (still two lower bars), eight more M4 x 12, and one M20 gland are needed, plus a bending brake in the shop. The door cable and the E-stop loop now reach the door plate from different sides of the box. `RunChecks()` compares heights now, so a flange may pass under a part's overhang. A DIN rail or duct screw through the upper plate hangs its nyloc and tail 9.4 mm below it, deeper than the 3 mm gap over the wall, so sheet 5 lists bands about 5 mm either side of the wall's top edge where no such screw may go (`ThroughBoltKeepOut()`). Everywhere else, `HangGaps()` keeps those nuts and tails `PartClear` over the lower deck's parts (issue #152). Each such screw sits at least 15 mm from the ends of its rail or duct, which also keeps it off the mid ring and its corner brackets. With the upper plate's 0.9 mm sag and the 106 mm bay, the tightest case is a rail screw over K2, at 20 mm.
Revisit if: the PSU's terminals do not face the 110 V rail (move the pass-through); the door plate sits at the back or at the left wall (reconsider the exit); the shop has no brake; a test bend shows the K factor is far from 0.42; or a measured corner bracket leaves no 4 mm key path past B's tab to the screws of the front right bracket from the lower post to the mid ring, which goes in after the wall (lower the tab); or the shop's brake cannot take the folded flange past its end within 12.8 mm of the tab's bend (raise `CornerFoot`; the opening stays 5.2 mm wide); or the shop has a press brake with segmented dies (it could close the corner as Rejected describes); or the PSU's terminals face the front, about 10 mm from B's open corner (close the corner).
