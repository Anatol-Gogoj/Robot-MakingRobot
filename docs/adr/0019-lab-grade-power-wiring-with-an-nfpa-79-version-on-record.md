# ADR-0019: Lab-grade power wiring now: copper on every power branch and trunk fuses, with an NFPA 79 version on record
Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-10-22: the NFPA 79 proposal approved "as a parallel version", the box built lab-grade); Date: 2026-10-22; Scope: `tools/draw_wiring.py` (`PartSize["F3"]`, `R24Rail0`, `LowerRails()`, `LowerWiring()`, sheet 1's V+ trunks, the sheet 2 and 3 notes, sheet 2C, `BomRows()`), `tools/layout_study/` (the R24 block and its nets, `layout3b_build.json`), `docs/wiring` sheets 1, 2A to 2C and 3, `bom_frame.csv`, `CAD/Control Box`; issue #205
Context: NFPA 79 (2024) was read on 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22 against the drawings and the parts on hand. The drawings put the driver feeds F2-1 to F2-7 (5 A) on 18 AWG, the ODrive feed F2-8 (10 A) on 16 AWG and everything else on 20 AWG. No 18, 16 or 14 AWG wire was ever bought. The 20 AWG on hand (Iron Forge B09CFWQQFR, 2026-09-14 order) is copper-clad aluminum automotive wire whose listing rules out 110/220 VAC; it conducts like 22 AWG copper, 3 A in Table 12.5.1. The FB modules (OONO B08PT47SFD) take unlisted ATO/ATC blade fuses, rated 32 V DC, and their input is rated 30 A, while the 1200 W PSU can deliver 50 A; the 12 AWG runs from the PSU to FB1 and through K2 to FB2 had no fuse. NFPA 79 asks for copper only (12.2.1, whose one exception is aluminum busbars), listed insulation types or qualifying AWM (12.3.1, 12.9.2), power circuits of 14 AWG or more unless listed or Class CC, J, CF or T fuses protect smaller ones (12.6.1 to 12.6.1.2), control circuits of 18 AWG or more in an enclosure (12.6.3), protection wherever a conductor can carry more than its ampacity (7.2.1.1, 7.2.3), and a posted key for any color scheme other than its defaults (13.2.4.4).
Decision: Build the box lab-grade, on two rules: every conductor is rated for the fuse or the source that feeds it (Table 12.5.1, 60 C column, since the terminal ratings are unknown), and its insulation for its voltage. So F2-1 to F2-8, the servo 6 V pair and the Pi 5 V pair and their 0 V returns run on 14 AWG copper THHN; the F0 rung inside the box on 18 AWG UL 1015, 600 V; and the 20 AWG on hand only on signals and on loads fused at 3 A or less. Each PSU trunk gets its own fuse, F3: a BAOMAIN RT18-32(X) 3-pole DIN holder (B08XNZ4DG3, the owner's pick) with 10 x 38 mm DC fuses, F3-1 25 A from PSU V+ to K2 (FB2), F3-2 10 A to FB1, F3-3 spare. F3 stands first on the 24 V rail, whose front end moves from x 87 to 32: F3 clears cable slot 3 by 1 mm, K2 and the bars move 3 mm back and the servo PSU with them, 1.9 mm from cable slot 2. Colors stay as drawn: blue for 0 V, never plain white, since the PSU's V- lead shares the mains duct and terminal strip with the white neutral. Layout 3b is rescored, not re-solved: 36,839 conductor-mm at the study's 85.5 mm bay (was 36,368) and 37,494 as drawn at 106 mm (was 37,024), +1.3 %, partly because the FB1 trunk now passes the 24 V rail. The NFPA 79 version stays on record, not drawn: every power conductor 14 AWG copper or more; control circuits 18 AWG copper and electronic I/O 22 AWG copper, as MTW or as UL 1015 AWM with VW-1, 30 mil insulation and a printed legend (12.9.2); no copper-clad aluminum anywhere; blue 0 V with a white band at each end under 13.2.3.2 Exception 2; and a color key posted inside the box (13.2.4.4), since black is mains L, red +24 V and blue 0 V, where 13.2.4.3's defaults are black for DC power and blue for DC control. Two departures remain even in that version: unlisted blade fuses protect the 24 V power branches, which 7.2.1.3 allows only if those are not branch circuits, and with a laptop on the Mega's USB the Mega's I/O stays live with Q0 off, which 13.2.4.1 would mark in orange.
Rejected: Keeping the 18 and 16 AWG as drawn: never bought, and with blade fuses 12.6.1 rules them out. Class CC fuse holders on the eleven power branches: the cost and rail space of eleven holders. Inline blade fuse holders for the trunks: a splice at each holder and nothing on a rail. F3 at the 24 V rail's back end: it pushes the servo PSU onto cable slot 2 and the base PE stud. The servo PSU moved into the free front corner instead: it closes the lane from slot 3 to K2's top face. Re-solving the lower deck with CP-SAT: OR-Tools is not installed here, and ADR-0018's reason holds, a new arrangement redraws every sheet for a 1.3 % shorter score at best.
Consequences: New spend about $98 (BoM, prices read 2026-10-22): 14 AWG THHN in red and blue, 50 ft each (about 32 and 28 ft needed, 42 and 37 for the NFPA 79 version), 18 AWG UL 1015 in black and white, the F3 holder and two packs of 10 x 38 mm fuses. The blade fuses on hand cover every FB1 and FB2 value. F3's size is assumed (54 x 82 x 66, from 77 to 79 x 60 to 61 mm per RT18-32 pole at retailers), so it is dashed on sheet 3 and translucent in the model. In front of F3 the lane from slot 3 to K2's top face is 9 mm wide in plan; its wires stack in height. The plates, panels and their DXFs do not change. The ferrule test fit is still open: 14 AWG lands only if a 2.5 mm2 ferrule fits the TB6600 power terminals and the SSR sockets.
Revisit if: the 2.5 mm2 ferrule does not fit (then 16 AWG, still rated for every branch fuse); F3 measures deeper than 82 mm or taller than 66; a load on the 20 AWG gets a fuse over 3 A; or the box has to meet NFPA 79, in which case apply the version above and record the two remaining departures.
