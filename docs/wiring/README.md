# Control panel wiring drawings

Six legal-size pages (14 x 8.5 in, landscape: sheets 1, 2A, 2B, 3, 4 and 5) of the planned layered E-stop
control panel, and the two deck plates and the side and top panels as DXF:

| File | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `wiring_schematic_legal.pdf` | Sheet 1: every circuit on one page: mains and K1, PSU, K2 and the E-stop latch, FB1 and FB2 with fuse values, the seven drivers and the ODrive, Mega and Pi I/O, star ground S and PE. |
| `wiring_din_rails_legal.pdf` | Sheet 2, two pages (`wiring_din_rails_lower_legal.svg`, `wiring_din_rails_upper_legal.svg`): sheet 1's circuits on the box, 2A the lower deck and 2B the upper, so that each part, terminal block and wire can be placed. Each deck is seen from above with the front at the left, so the left wall is at the top; sheet 3 draws its plans mirrored, as seen from below. Every rail, part, duct, cable slot, grommet and PE stud comes from the generator's layout (`DeckSolids()`, `Ducts()`, `CableSlots`, `CableEntries`, `PeStud`), so each part sits where sheet 3 puts it and moves with it. Every wire runs from terminal to terminal through the ducts and the free channels between the rails, leaving a terminal at right angles and turning only where two of them meet. A wire that changes deck ends in its cable slot on both pages, and the table on each page names its two ends (`SlotWires()`); a cable that leaves the box ends at its grommet, the IEC inlet or the door cable's gland, with what it carries written beside it. Ducts and channels are drawn as wide as their wires need and the rest narrower, so the parts keep their order and their places relative to each other but not their exact distances: measure from sheet 3. `MainsWireChecks()` keeps every 110 VAC wire in the compartment's lanes and the mains duct, the PSU's own DC leads in the mains duct (with a separator), and every other wire but PE out of all of them. `CrossChecks()` refuses a wire through a part, two parts that overlap, and a corner or end of one wire that touches another, which would read as a joint. The supplies are the owner's of 2026-10-15 (ADR-0016): the Pi at the operator panel on the 5 V PSU, the Mega on the Pi's USB, the screen on the Pi, the ODrive in the spin coater's base. |

The lower bay is 106 mm because the lower left grommet sets it (ADR-0011). FB1 is the 12-way module that was delivered, so the fuse rail is 60 mm longer than in the study; the 5 V PSU sits in the 24 V row against the back ring, and the lower left grommet past the fuse rail's end (ADR-0014). The 110 VAC compartment has no wall (ADR-0013): its edge, a line 10 mm outside the mains parts, is drawn dashed, and every low-voltage part stays outside it. |

The design comes from the control panel wiring reference of 2026-10-06, with the supplies of 2026-10-15
(ADR-0016). It is the planned panel, not the machine as it is wired today. Open items (tower common
polarity, lid feed, valve coil, servo 6 V rating, which side powers ODrive J11 logic, the 5 V PSU's rating
for the Pi 5) are listed in sheet 1's notes, with the relay off-drive on pins 4 and 42 (issue #131).

Every wire is drawn with horizontal and vertical segments only; `Sheet.Wire()` in the generator refuses a
diagonal. Colors are Paul Tol's vibrant scheme.

## Print

Paper: Legal. Orientation: landscape. Scale: 100 % (actual size), not "fit to page". The drawings keep a
4.5 mm margin inside the paper edge.

## Regenerate

```bash
python tools/draw_wiring.py
```

That rewrites the six SVGs, the six DXFs and the BoM here, reads each panel DXF back to check its webs, and
lists the panel cut-outs that are still placeholders (`PanelOpen()`). Sheets 3 and 4 and the DXFs share one set
of frame dimensions in the generator (`BoxD`, `LowBay`, `DeckSolids()`, `PlateGeom()`, `PanelGeom()` and the
constants around them), so a change there moves all of them together, and sheet 2 routes its wires again on
the moved parts. To make the PDFs, open each SVG in Edge or Chrome and print it to PDF on
Legal landscape with no margins, or use headless Edge with `--print-to-pdf` on a page whose
`@page` size is `14in 8.5in`; sheet 2's PDF holds its two SVGs, one per page. Print through Edge: PyMuPDF does not
apply the SVGs' style sheet, and its wires come out as black fills.

## 3D block model

`CAD/Control Box/ControlBoxBlocks.FCStd`, with a `.step` export beside it, is the box in 3D as blocks: the
frame in its 2020 profile with the 32 corner brackets, both plates with every hole and cable slot, each DIN
rail, part and duct of `DeckSolids()` with the printed clips, the panels with their grommets, vents and the door gland, and the
110 VAC zone as a translucent volume. Nothing in it is drawn by hand; to rebuild it after a change in the
generator, run this in the FreeCAD 1.x Python console:

```python
import runpy; runpy.run_path(r"<repo>/tools/build_box_model.py", run_name="__main__")
```

It rebuilds the document, refuses to save if two solids overlap, and writes both files. Since issue #158 part A the builder also adds the four gantry mount brackets to the lower module and the gantry members around the box (sheet 5, drawn 60 mm past it on each side). The lower module,
the upper module and its lid are separate `App::Part` containers, so raising one shows the box exploded.
A deck part whose size is assumed (dashed on sheet 3) is translucent, and every solid carries a `SizeSource`
property. FreeCAD's axes are right-handed and the generator's x (front to back) and y (left to right) are
not, so the model takes the generator's y as X and its x as Y; FreeCAD's Front view shows the box's front.

```bash
python tools/check_wiring_pins.py
```

That checks every Mega pin of the generator against `pins_RAMPS_14_RMR.h`, `pins_RAMPS.h` and
`Configuration_adv.h`, confirms each pin label is present on sheet 1, and asks the generator whether each
pin is a terminal of the Mega on sheet 2B with a wire on it (a word search there would pass on terminal
numbers alone).
