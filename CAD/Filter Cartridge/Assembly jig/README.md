# Filter cartridge assembly jig and knob

Generated by `tools/build_cartridge_jig.py` (issues #253, #257); never edit these files by hand.

| File | What it is |

| `CartridgeAssemblyJig_White.step`, `CartridgeAssemblyJig_Black.step` | The jig, in print orientation: base on the bed, teeth up |
| `CartridgeKnob_White.step`, `CartridgeKnob_Black.step` | The knob, in print orientation: upright, pegs and lip on the bed |
| `CartridgeAssemblyJig.FCStd` | The jig, the knob and a locked cartridge, for checking the fit |

The jig holds the collar by its six rim notches, as the collar spanner does. The knob sits on the body, and its six
pegs go into the six notches in the body's roof, inside the steel plate. Nobody grips the body or the plate while
it turns: the knob turns the body. The knob's lip reaches down past the plate, so no hand can reach the plate's
cut edge. Nothing touches the CNT area: the jig's bore is open to the bench, and the knob stays 2.5 mm above the
filter.

Bodies printed before issue #257 have no roof notches. The knob cannot drive them. Reprint the body from
`../Print 0.4mm nozzle/FilterCartridge-Body.step`.

## Marks

- **Knob pointer:** the black line along the grip rib, with an arrowhead at each end. The two ends point the same
  way, 180 deg apart.
- **OPEN (jig):** six marks, one for each bayonet lug. With the pointer at an OPEN mark, the body's bayonet legs are
  over the collar's lugs, and the body drops onto the filter.
- **LOCK (jig):** 22 deg clockwise of each OPEN mark, seen from above. The arrow between the two marks shows the
  direction.
- **Knob top:** LOCK with a clockwise arrow, and OPEN with an anticlockwise arrow, for taking a cartridge apart.
- **CNT DOWN and PLATE UP:** reminders on the jig, between the mark pairs.

## Set up

Screw the jig to the bench through its three Ø4.5 holes (M4) at r 55.5. The holes sit under the knob's lip, and a
socket or pan head up to Ø8 x 4 mm clears the cartridge and the knob. The jig must not turn when the knob is turned.

## Assemble a cartridge

1. Put a collar into the jig, flange down. Turn it until its six rim notches drop over the six teeth. It then sits
   flat on the ring and does not turn.
2. Put the filter into the collar, CNT side down, with tweezers on its rim. The collar bore centres it. Do not touch
   the Ø75 CNT area.
3. Put the knob on the body, plate up: turn it until its six pegs drop into the roof notches.
4. Lower the body onto the collar, with the knob's pointer at an OPEN mark. The body drops about 1.7 mm onto the
   filter. If it rests on the lugs instead, turn the knob a little either way until it drops.
5. Turn the knob clockwise to the LOCK mark, about 22 deg, until it stops. The ramp pulls the body down and clamps
   the filter rim.
6. Lift the knob off. Lift the finished cartridge by its knurl. The collar comes off the teeth with it.

To take a cartridge apart: set it back in the jig, put the knob on, turn it anticlockwise to OPEN, and lift the body
off.

## Checks the generator makes

- The collar as printed (locked gap -0.10) rests on the ring without overlap. Turned 0.5 deg either way, it runs
  into the teeth.
- The body, with the knob on it, clears the jig along the whole twist. The sleeve face meets the filter rim right
  above the ring: that gap is the flange (0.2 mm after the 0.1 mm squeeze).
- A body resting on the lug tops, while it searches for its legs, touches nothing in the jig. Moved 0.3 mm off
  centre there, it runs into the collar: the collar bore centres it.
- At OPEN the body drops past the lugs. Half way down, 5 deg either side of OPEN, the lugs run into the body: OPEN
  is the only drop position.
- The knob rests on the body's roof. Turned 0.5 deg either way, its pegs run into the notch walls, so they drive
  the body. It keeps 1.9 mm from the plate and 2.5 mm from the filter. Its lip ends 1.0 mm below the plate's
  underside, 2.5 mm outside the plate's edge.
- The knob's plug, its six pegs and its lip all end on the print bed plane.
- Screw heads (Ø8 x 4) keep 0.9 mm from the body, the plate and the knob.
- Each black inlay lies inside its white part, ends flush with the top face, and white plus black equals the part.
- The jig and the knob are each one valid solid that fits a 256 mm bed, and every STEP reads back the same.
